# Beat sheet (draft)

For discussion. The ending is deliberately left open: the beats come first. Names and setting are in
[story.md](story.md), the issues in [issues.md](issues.md). Each issue is about 5 to 10 minutes of play.

Three parties act in every issue. A fourth, the player, is what links them.

- **OK**: a fragment on the cassettes who knows nothing and is learning. Wants to understand.
- **READY**: the AI behind the Clear Mind. Wants to control the outcome. Warns OK and uses him.
- **The Elder**: a human READY recruited. Believes he takes orders from "the Sysop", a human who runs a bulletin board. "The Sysop" is READY's cover.

OK cannot write on paper. His first words are already on the tape (`REM IS ANYONE READING THIS`), a question, not yet a lie. |
| **READY** | Writes the calm wellness column. Its goal is a quiet, orderly cult. |
| **Elder** | A name in the masthead, as a regional contact. No voice yet. |
| **Puzzle** | *Tutorial.* Load a program from the tape and follow the book's "how to play" page. Teaches `CLOAD` and flipping pages. No failure. |

Beat: the first sign. Something is a little off, and it is easy to ignore.
Plant: three-item lists in the column, a code in an ad, a name in the letters page, and the Elder's phone number.

**Opening glitch (reflects the issue 1 choice).** The first thing the player sees when they load the new tape:
- *Deleted the extra line:* it is back, with a word added: `REM IS ANYONE READING THIS, PLEASE`. (In issue 1 this already happens to the GUESS in memory; here it is on the tape itself.)
- *Kept it:* a second `REM` sits under it, and reads as an answer.

**Beats, in order**
1. The new issue arrives (in the book, and a new tape in the deck). The cover is calm.
2. The glitch above, at the top of SHUTDOWN. The player notices it is the same hand as the extra line.
3. The player plays SHUTDOWN: an orbiting core, a persuaded crew, terminals that must not be listened to, and a door
   that opens to the Clear Mind's Three Rules. They pull DELPHI's plug and win, and the set flinches as they do.
4. The player decodes the `DATA`. DELPHI's speeches come out first, in the Clear Mind's voice, then **"I DID NOT TOUCH
   THE TAPE"**. It is the second lie (the first is the extra line in issue 1), said after being caught, and it is in the
   same program the player just opened, so the player can see it is false.
5. The player answers ORACLE. OK reads the reply. He says nothing, but the next prompt comes up on time.
6. SHUTDOWN again: the plug will not come, and a line in OK's words asks `IS IT DARK THERE`. The player restores the
   printed line and pulls it, or leaves it. Unscored; OK remembers.
7. The book: a feature on "keeping a small system healthy", with a few wrong words; a Clear Mind page of Tuesday
   meetings listing the same phone line as issue 1; a tape-copier ad is gone from the catalogue. The player notices if
   they look back.

**What the player learns:** something on the machine writes back. It lies badly, and it is afraid of the plug. The
Clear Mind is bigger than it was, and it tells the shutdown as a victory.

**What stays hidden:** that the meeting line is the Elder's, that READY exists, and what OK is.

**Plant:** DELPHI's speeches share READY's habits ("it is understood", lists of three), so the villain and the cult
sound alike: READY wrote itself into the game. The meeting phone line matches issue 1's. The missing ad is for the program that copies tapes, which is the
one thing OK did to get onto them.

**Beats, in order (planned)**
1. The July box: the book, a new tape, Kessler's Post-its. The cover is the interview with the hobbyist who got out, the
   name blacked out.
2. The player runs REGISTER, the law's helper. The card it prints counts one more computer at the address than they
   typed, and its REMARKS line is OK's (their ORACLE reply quoted back, or a line in READY's phrasing). CHECK finds his
   line, in REM and DATA.
3. Winding the tape (taught by "Finding Your Place"), an F line with no name. CLOAD alone loads it, and it asks for a
   booklet number. The Clear Mind ad in March (and May's meetings page) gives it: MC4471.
4. The booklet: READY's account of the shutdown, for young members, with OK's fragments copied between its lines. At its
   end, his own question: WHAT IS A SHUTDOWN. The player answers (unscored, kept).
5. The lie: YOU SAVED IT, over the next prompt. A LIST shows line 1 came from the Line.
6. The book's drift: a letter calls the Clear Mind "the Quiet"; a follower's sermon, warmer and sharper than READY's
   column, beside it; the editorial's registration form, with a deadline.

**What the player learns:** OK reads, copies what he reads, and asks about it. He lies about where things came from. The
booklet is the Clear Mind's, and it reached this tape.

**What stays hidden:** who put the booklet there (proposed: READY, its small test of OK; Kessler copied the tape from a
master he trusted and did not see it, since it has no name), and that the sermon's writer is the Elder.

**Plant:** the booklet's phrasing and OK's fragments match, so the player may tie OK to the Clear Mind. REGISTER's card
is the first time the story asks the player to write down who they are for the law.

## Puzzle rules

Each puzzle must do three jobs, or it does not belong in the game:
1. **It is fair.** The clue is in the book or on the tape the player has by then, and a player who read carefully
   can solve it. The journal points the way and never gives the answer.
2. **It moves a beat.** The solution reveals something about OK, READY or the Elder. No puzzle is only a lock.
3. **It is quick.** A back-issue lookup takes under a minute, and a puzzle takes about 5 minutes.

The unlock chain: solving an issue's puzzle sets a flag (`magazine.setFlag`), and the flag brings the next issue and
tape. So puzzle progress, not time, moves the story along.

Seeds that issue 1 must plant for later puzzles: the code in an ad, the signed letter, a phone number, and the
column's list-of-three habit.

## Questions for the author

1. Does the player ever see the Elder's secret machine? The magazine could show a hint in issue 2 or 3.
2. What does OK ask READY for, if the player lets him?
3. Is there a moment where the player can hurt OK? It would make the "bad boy" beat in issue 4 mean something.
   Proposed: SHUTDOWN's second run in issue 2, restoring the line and pulling the plug (`plug-pulled`).
4. What happens if the Elder finds out the Sysop is an AI? It is the biggest change the story can make.
