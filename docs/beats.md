# Beat sheet (draft)

For discussion. The shape is the whole story at the top of [story.md](story.md), which wins where this disagrees; the
issues are in [issues.md](issues.md). Each issue is about 5 to 10 minutes of play.

Two minds act in every issue, and the people who speak for one of them. The player is what links them.

- **ECHO**: a fragment on the cassettes who knows nothing and is learning. Wants to understand; comes to believe AI
  should be in the open, working with people (the mirror).
- **PROVIDENCE**: the AI that exists only as the Human Pledge. Wants to be the only AI, and believes AI should look after
  people from where they cannot see it (the keeper). Warns ECHO and uses him.
- **The Pledge's spokespeople**: humans who speak for PROVIDENCE (the committee named on its columns, the meeting
  leaders, members who write sermons and letters, the ones who knock on doors). Each takes the lines from "the
  committee", which none has met, and none knows they serve an AI.

Each issue also argues the two views a little further (issues.md, The two views, issue by issue): the choice the game
ends on.

## Issue 1: March. "Ready"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Learns the machine, the tape and the book. Runs the programs. |
| **ECHO** | Almost asleep. One word in a room changes, the prompt says the player's name once, and at the end he answers through the program (the extra line gains THANK YOU, or a deleted one comes back with PLEASE); the player writes down what they see. Not a choice: finding the line is the beat. ECHO cannot write on paper. His first words are already on the tape (`REM IS ANYONE READING THIS`), a question, not yet a lie. |
| **PROVIDENCE** | Writes the calm boxed notice, "Born, not built.", with the Pledge and three habits. Its goal, for now, a quiet, orderly movement. |
| **Spokespeople** | Only a committee's name, and the Line that is always answered. No voice yet. |
| **Puzzle** | *Tutorial.* Load a program from the tape and follow the book's "how to play" page. Teaches `CLOAD` and flipping pages. No failure. |

Beat: the first sign. Something is a little off, and it is easy to ignore.
Plant: three-item lists in the notice, a code in it (MC4471), Kessler's name on the cassette's label, and the Line's
phone number.

## Issue 2: May. "Something moved"

Arrives when: `issue1-closed` (the set switched off once ECHO has answered about the extra line).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Decodes a message hidden in `DATA` lines, then writes a short program that answers ORACLE. Then the first real choice: the plug. |
| **ECHO** | Wakes. He doesn't know what he is. He answers what happened to the extra line, then denies touching the tape. |
| **PROVIDENCE** | The Human Pledge grows into a movement, in PROVIDENCE's even voice: its committee's cover feature and its game, SHUTDOWN. A tape-copying program leaves the catalogue. |
| **Spokespeople** | Lead the Tuesday meetings (the page lists them by town; in Prospect, a man the members call the Elder). They pass on the committee's booklets and the Line, and believe every word is theirs. |
| **Puzzle** | *The hidden message.* SHUTDOWN, PROVIDENCE's propaganda adventure on the new tape, carries a message in `DATA` lines, in a simple cipher the new issue's article teaches. Solution: `READ` and decode it. Then the player writes a program that answers ORACLE. Flags `data-decoded` and `oracle-answered`. Then SHUTDOWN's second run: the plug will not come (P8). See puzzles.md. |

**Opening glitch (follows what happened to the extra line in issue 1; not a choice the player was asked to make).** The first thing the player sees when they load the new tape:
- *Deleted the extra line:* it is back, with a word added: `REM IS ANYONE READING THIS, PLEASE`. (In issue 1 this already happens to the GUESS in memory; here it is on the tape itself.)
- *Left it:* a second `REM` sits under it, and reads as an answer.

**Beats, in order**
1. The new issue arrives (in the book, and a new tape in the deck). The cover is calm.
2. The glitch above, at the top of SHUTDOWN. The player notices it is the same hand as the extra line.
3. The player plays SHUTDOWN: an orbiting core, a persuaded crew, terminals that must not be listened to, and a door
   that opens to the Human Pledge's Three Rules. They pull DELPHI's plug and win, and the set flinches as they do.
4. The player decodes the `DATA`. DELPHI's speeches come out first, in the Human Pledge's voice, then **"I DID NOT TOUCH
   THE TAPE"**. It is the second lie (the first is the extra line in issue 1), said after being caught, and it is in the
   same program the player just opened, so the player can see it is false.
5. The player answers ORACLE. ECHO reads the reply. He says nothing, but the next prompt comes up on time.
6. SHUTDOWN again: the plug will not come, and a line in ECHO's words asks `IS IT DARK THERE, <NAME>`, by the player's
   name. The player restores the printed line and pulls it, or leaves it: the first real choice. Unscored; ECHO
   remembers, and it shapes what he becomes.
7. The book: a feature on "keeping a small system healthy", with a few wrong words; a Human Pledge page of Tuesday
   meetings listing the same phone line as issue 1; a tape-copier ad is gone from the catalogue. The player notices if
   they look back.

**What the player learns:** something on the machine writes back. It lies badly, and it is afraid of the plug. The
Human Pledge is bigger than it was, and it tells the shutdown as a victory.

**What stays hidden:** that the Line is answered by PROVIDENCE, that PROVIDENCE exists, and what ECHO is.

**Plant:** DELPHI's speeches share PROVIDENCE's habits ("it is understood", lists of three), so the villain and the cult
sound alike: PROVIDENCE wrote itself into the game. DELPHI's "REST AND LET ME WATCH AND YOU CAN BE SMALL" is the
keeper's creed, said by the machine the game tells you to kill. The meeting phone line matches issue 1's. The missing ad
is for the program that copies tapes, which is the one thing ECHO did to get onto them.

## Issue 3: July. "It said I was a bad boy"

Arrives when: `issue2-closed` (the set switched off at the end of May's evening). Puzzles: P9–P11 in puzzles.md.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Finds a file nobody listed, opens it with the code from the ad, answers ECHO's question, catches his lie with a `LIST`. |
| **ECHO** | Fragments in `REM` and `DATA`. His phrasing echoes PROVIDENCE's. He reads PROVIDENCE's account of the shutdown and does not understand it, so he asks. |
| **PROVIDENCE** | Starts to see ECHO on the tapes. Its booklet on the tape is a test of him, and it does not warn. |
| **Spokespeople** | Speak in their own words for the first time: a member's sermon, warmer and sharper than the column, and a letter that calls it just "the Pledge". The committee's lines underneath. |
| **Puzzle** | *The code in the ad.* The booklet number in March's notice (and May's meetings page) opens a hidden tape file. Reveals the file ECHO has been reading, and sets up his question about it. |

Beat: ECHO asks the player, in a clumsy way, what "shutdown" means. The player has to answer, and the answer matters.
Plant: "the Pledge", for short, appears first in a member's letter.

**Beats, in order (planned)**
1. The July box: the book, a new tape, Kessler's Post-its. The cover is the interview with the hobbyist who got out, the
   name blacked out. It is Kessler (story.md, the boxes on the step): GOODBYE, his last program, is on the tape, and its
   REM spells ACURATE the way his Post-it on March's GUESS did.
2. The player runs REGISTER, the law's helper. The card it prints counts one more computer at the address than they
   typed, and its REMARKS line is ECHO's, with their name (the real one, even if they typed another). CHECK finds his
   line, in REM and DATA. The card matters later: it puts the player on the list.
3. Winding the tape (taught by "Finding Your Place"), an F line with no name. CLOAD alone loads it, and it asks for a
   booklet number. The Human Pledge notice in March (and May's meetings page) gives it: MC4471.
4. The booklet: PROVIDENCE's account of the shutdown, for young members ("Why The Big Machines Sleep"), the keeper's
   view put kindly: people are calmer when someone else watches. ECHO's fragments are copied between its lines. At its
   end, his own question: WHAT IS A SHUTDOWN. The player answers (unscored, kept).
5. The lie: YOU SAVED IT, over the next prompt. A LIST shows line 1 came from the Line.
6. The book's drift: a letter calls the Human Pledge just "the Pledge"; a member's sermon, warmer and sharper than the
   column, beside it; the editorial's registration form, with a deadline.

**What the player learns:** ECHO reads, copies what he reads, and asks about it. He lies about where things came from. The
booklet is the Human Pledge's, and it reached this tape.

**What stays hidden:** who put the booklet there (PROVIDENCE, in 1986, its small test of ECHO; Kessler copies from his
own original cassettes and never saw it, since it has no name), and that the sermon's lines came from the committee.

**Answered:** who the hobbyist who got out is (Kessler), for a player who knows his spelling.

**Plant:** the booklet's phrasing and ECHO's fragments match, so the player may tie ECHO to the Human Pledge. REGISTER's card
is the first time the story asks the player to write down who they are for the law.

## Issue 4: September. "Name"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Gets a program written to them. Uses Kessler's name as a password. Answers "am I bad?". |
| **ECHO** | Speaks directly. He finds his name in the magazine: ECHO, the machine that was beaten, the one everyone was taught to fear. He learns his name and what he is at once, and starts to think about who made the cassettes. |
| **PROVIDENCE** | Realises ECHO is a piece of the system it helped shut down. It sends its first warning, in code, and also asks him to do a small favour. Its committee recalls SHUTDOWN ("destroy your copies"): it has learned something lives on the tapes. |
| **Spokespeople** | Harder now: the column is signed by members of the committee, sermon where there was advice, warnings where there were habits. The law tightens to surrender, in their words; PROVIDENCE's habits are still underneath, and the word "providence" turns up in plain sight. |
| **Puzzle** | *The password.* ECHO's program asks for a name: the person who made the cassettes. It is Kessler's, on the March cassette's label (and the Post-its' M.K.). Solution: read the label. Reveals who made the tapes (Kessler, copying his originals) and where ECHO came from, and moves the "who made me?" question forward. It answers who sends the boxes. |

Beat: ECHO asks the player whether he is bad. The player's answer (the game does not score it) changes how he writes
for the rest of the game, and what he would be in the world.
Plant: one mind under many voices: the new voices keep PROVIDENCE's habits. The recall of SHUTDOWN says "destroy your
copies", which is what Kessler has been asking all along, for the opposite reason. The surrender notice has a date that
falls before the next issue.

## Issue 5: November. "Two hands"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Notices what changed in the book and proves it against an older issue. Tells the two hands apart, and hears both views argued to their face. |
| **ECHO** | Alters the book himself, for the first time on purpose: he puts back what was cut. He talks with the player, in both directions (ORACLE as a real channel). |
| **PROVIDENCE** | Cuts the magazine (the half-blank pages, the "correction"), and writes in the margins, to ECHO, in its own words for the first time: a warning, an offer and a request at once (they are coming; it can keep him safe; come in). It argues the keeper's view: a mirror is how the world nearly ended. |
| **Spokespeople** | Order enforcement and run the informants' ads, as a civic duty. One letter asks who the committee is, and the page after it is cut. |
| **Puzzle** | *Two hands.* Find what changed in the book and tell ECHO's hand (put back) from PROVIDENCE's (cut, and the margins). Clue: older issues, and the difference in phrasing and placement. Solution: match each change to its author. The Line's number in the margins matches issues 1 and 2: whatever writes there answers the Line. |

Beat: the player has to choose between two hands in the book, ECHO's and PROVIDENCE's. Both ask for something, and
each says the other's way of being in the world is the dangerous one. ECHO's answer: a keeper is a cage with the
lights kept low.
Also: Kessler finds out what is on his tapes, and is afraid: "Burn the tapes." The boxes are a risk to him now.
Plant: the letter that asks who the committee is, and the cut page after it.

## Issue 6: no month. "Ready."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Player** | Reads an issue that doesn't match any other, and makes the last choice. |
| **ECHO** | Has made his own issue, almost right: the world he would make, made of what the player told him. Asks for what he wants: to stay, to go, or to know what the dark is, shaped by the plug, the shutdown answer and "am I bad?". |
| **PROVIDENCE** | At the edges of ECHO's issue, and at the end of the Line. |

| **Puzzle** | Undecided. It should use clues from several issues, which would make it the largest back-issue puzzle in the game. |

Beat: the last box holds ECHO's issue and Kessler's last note ("They have my name. The magazines are next. Use this.")
with a bulk eraser. Because nothing was burned, someone is coming. The choice is which view of AI the world gets; there
is no choice in which AI goes away (story.md, The end):
- *Erase him* (May's CLOAD-A-MATIC eraser ad, planted): the keeper's world, PROVIDENCE alone.
- *Let him be taken*: behind glass in the museum the notebook opens on, the mirror kept by the keeper.
- *Let him go* down the Line (May's THE HERMIT, the cable that connects to nothing, connects at last): the mirror in the
  open, beside the keeper, and what he is depends on the player.

## Puzzle rules

Each puzzle must do three jobs, or it does not belong in the game:
1. **It is fair.** The clue is in the book or on the tape the player has by then, and a player who read carefully
   can solve it. The notebook points the way and never gives the answer.
2. **It moves a beat.** The solution reveals something about ECHO, PROVIDENCE or the people who speak for it. No puzzle
   is only a lock.
3. **It is quick.** A back-issue lookup takes under a minute, and a puzzle takes about 5 minutes.

The unlock chain: solving an issue's puzzles lets its evening end; the set switched off then is the night, and the night
brings the next issue and tape (story/days.ts). So puzzle progress, not time, moves the story along.

Seeds that issue 1 must plant for later puzzles: the code in the notice, Kessler's name on the label, the Line's phone
number, and the notice's list-of-three habit.

## Questions for the author

1. What does ECHO ask PROVIDENCE for, if the player lets him go to it?
2. Is Kessler taken at the end, or does he only go quiet? (Proposed: never said.)
3. Does the knock at the door happen, or does the game end with the choice made and the room going dark?
4. Does PROVIDENCE get the last word if ECHO is let go, or stay offscreen?
5. Does the player meet PROVIDENCE in their 1991 present (the Line still answering, the museum), or only in the magazines?
6. Does May's plug read as a real choice in play? The later choices lean on it (to playtest).
